The full timeline — from Taproot to Rune #3 ↓
Nov 2021

Taproot activates on Bitcoin

A soft fork expanded the witness data space and created the groundwork for everything that followed.

Jan 2023

Ordinals (Casey Rodarmor)

Casey's protocol lets people inscribe images, text and art onto individual sats, directly on Bitcoin. Source

Mar 2023

BRC 20, the developer "domo"

Domo creates BRC 20 using Ordinals inscriptions as a way to deploy, mint and transfer tokens on Bitcoin. Powerful, but it bloats the UTXO set with unspendable junk. Source

Nov 2023

Binance rushes to list ORDI

The price doubled within hours. Exchanges saw Bitcoin becoming an asset layer. Source

Dec 2023

The Runestone (Leonidas)

Conceived by Leonidas with art by Leo Caillard. It became the largest airdrop in Bitcoin history, with 112,383 inscriptions and a 3.97 MB parent inscription sealed in an address attributed to Satoshi. $DOG was later airdropped to its holders. Full story

Apr 2024

Runes (Casey Rodarmor), in the halving block

Casey fixes the bloat: Runes use the UTXO model plus OP RETURN, with no junk UTXOs. $DOG secures Rune #3. Source

25 Apr 2024

$DOG is born, Rune #3

Airdropped 100% to Runestone holders with no presale, no team, no owner and CC0. The biggest community token on Bitcoin. View on chain

What is $DOG?
$DOG (DOG•GO•TO•THE•MOON) is the largest community coin on Bitcoin. Technically it's Rune #3, etched inside Bitcoin's own network with the Runes protocol at block 840,000. Supply of 100 billion, open license (CC0), no owner. View on chain.
How was it created? Was it fair?
One of the fairest launches ever. No presale. No team reserve. No discounted investor. $DOG was airdropped free to those who contributed to the network. People active in the Bitcoin and Ordinals ecosystem earned the Runestone, and Runestone holders got the $DOG airdrop (~75,000 wallets). Even the creator took only ~0.008%. Source.
Who controls $DOG today?
No company. No CEO. No team wallet. $DOG is spread across tens of thousands of real people — the airdrop alone reached ~75,000 wallets, and the $DOG Army you see barking in the chat, funding plans and marching in the streets is who actually carries the coin. Diversified, human, verifiable. And because nobody should take that on faith, we also watch the exceptions: one cluster gathered about 12% of supply — documented, not proven, under daily watch. See the case and the watched wallets.
How do I store $DOG safely?
The most sovereign way is self custody. Take $DOG off the exchange and keep it in your own Bitcoin and Runes wallet that only you control. Pulling coins off the exchange shrinks the float that can be used against the holder. This is education, not financial advice.
Why "don't trust, verify"?
Because everything here is public and reproducible. You don't take our word for it. The data lives on dogdata.xyz, mempool.space and ordinals.com. See the sources and check it yourself.
